Why the headlines surged this week

Six days turned a valuation debate into a capex-trajectory debate. The trigger was not a data point about demand. It was the labs asking for a slowdown, on the same weekend the bond market pushed the 10-year to 5%.

  1. Thu Sep 10

    Capital Economics publishes a "late-stage bubble" screen: eight indicator sets at or near historic peaks, S&P 500 to 8,250 by year-end then down 30% or more in 2027. Oracle reports another quarter of negative free cash flow (−$5.4B on $28.5B capex) but a record $664B backlog; the stock rallies, then gives it all back.

  2. Fri Sep 11

    August CPI prints 3.4% headline on an energy shock (Brent near $108). Odds of a September Fed hike pass 85%. The 10-year reaches 4.97%.

  3. Sat Sep 12

    Dario Amodei publishes an essay urging frontier labs to pace capability gains. Within hours Sam Altman tells Fortune a 2026 OpenAI IPO would be "ill-advised" and hints at an industry pact to slow down. Musk posts agreement.

  4. Mon Sep 14

    Philadelphia Semiconductor Index −5.9%. Nvidia −3.4% (−$177B). SoftBank −13% in Tokyo. Micron −5.3%, Broadcom −4.8%, AMD −4.4%, Oracle −5%, CoreWeave −5%. The 10-year prints 5.01% intraday. Nvidia, Palantir and Booz Allen restrict use of Anthropic models over data retention. Michael Burry calls the slowdown talk "self-serving cover for slowing growth."

  5. Tue Sep 15

    Chips stabilize (SOX +0.4%). BofA's fund manager survey shows "disorderly rise in bond yields" displacing "AI bubble" as the top tail risk for the first time, with equity overweight falling from 56% to 49% and a record 33% saying companies are over-investing.

Reality check on the narrative itself

The article count did rise: at least 96 "AI bubble" items in Google News over seven days, 21 of them on Monday. But Google search interest is at 0.6× its 12-month average (27 versus an average of 45; the peak of 100 was November 2025, with a second peak of 84 in late May 2026), and GDELT's seven-day article volume is at 46% of its 12-month mean. This is the third or fourth wave of "bubble" coverage in a year, and by search volume a smaller one. Each prior wave passed without a capex cut.

The evidence, both ways

Hard data means prices, filings, guidance, deals or surveys. Opinion means strategist calls, CEO remarks and essays. Every item is from August 25 to September 15 unless dated otherwise.

Bear What says it is popping

  • The infrastructure layer is in a bear market. SOX −23.9%, Oracle −56%, Arm −46%, CoreWeave −42%, Super Micro −37%, Vertiv −37%, SoftBank −32%, Broadcom −28%, Micron −24% from 52-week highs. hard data
  • Rates broke the model. 10-year at 5.01% intraday, 30-year 5.33%, first Fed hike since 2022 priced at 88–91% for Wednesday. Oracle and CoreWeave fell on the yield spikes of Sep 1 and Sep 14 specifically. FRED hard data
  • Self-funding is over. Big-4 capex reached 97% of operating cash flow in Q2. Amazon's trailing free cash flow is −$7.6B; Alphabet posted its first negative quarter since IPO; Meta's Q2 FCF fell 91%. Debt funded 32% of capex over the last year versus 9% two years ago. Epoch AI, FactSet hard data
  • Credit fatigue. $220B of hyperscaler bonds year-to-date versus $121B in all of 2025; order books shrank from 5× to under 2×; 78 of 91 bonds trade wider than issue. A Vantage data-center ABS dropped a tranche; CoreWeave's loan flexed to 10.4%. MLQ, Asset Securitization Report hard data
  • Oracle is the weak link. BBB−, net debt $88B, FCF negative five quarters running, more than half of its $664B backlog reportedly from OpenAI, CDS at a record ~215 bp. 10-Q, S&P hard data
  • The funding window narrowed. OpenAI ruled out a 2026 IPO, deferring the liquidity event SoftBank ($65B committed), Nvidia and Amazon were financing against. SoftBank is marketing a $10–20B junk bond this week to carry the stake. Fortune via Yahoo hard data
  • Unit economics are deflating. Silicon Data's usage-weighted token price index fell 60% in Q2; H100 on-demand is ~70% below its peak; AWS cut GPU prices up to 45%. TechStartups, bex.co hard data
  • Positioning turned. Fund managers cut equity overweight from 56% to 49% in a month; long-semis crowding collapsed from 82% to 53% in August; CNN Fear & Greed went from 64 to 29 in a month; AAII bears now outnumber bulls. BofA FMS hard data
  • The labs blinked. Amodei's essay and Altman's "ill-advised" IPO comment converted a valuation debate into a capex-trajectory debate. Burry: "self-serving" cover for slowing growth. Capital Economics: late-stage bubble, S&P −30% in 2027. CNBC, Fortune opinion
  • Valuation is at 1999 altitude on one measure. Shiller CAPE 40.7 versus the December 1999 peak of 44.2; Magnificent 7 are 34% of the S&P 500, top ten 38%. multpl hard data

Bull What says it is not

  • Nvidia is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained. Q2 revenue $96.2B (+106%), data center $89B (+117%), 75% gross margin, Q3 guide $108B against $104.2B consensus with zero China assumed. CFO: bottleneck "through the end of fiscal 2028." Nvidia hard data
  • $2.35 trillion of contracted cloud backlog. Microsoft $678B (+84%), Oracle $664B, Google Cloud $514B, AWS $496B. All four say demand exceeds capacity; Jassy expects that to hold through 2027. Microsoft, Fortune hard data
  • The most current number in the supply chain is accelerating. TSMC's August revenue was a record NT$514.8B, +53.3% year over year, up from +44.7% in July. TSMC hard data
  • Server makers cannot ship fast enough. Dell: $60.9B of AI orders in one quarter, $95B backlog, guidance raised $25B. Super Micro: FY27 guide $65–72B versus $52.5B consensus. HPE: record $7.6B AI backlog, limited by memory supply. Dell, SMCI hard data
  • Revenue caught up with the story. Anthropic's run-rate went from $9B to $65B in seven months with positive adjusted operating income; OpenAI's passed $40B, roughly double year-end, with enterprise now larger than consumer. TechCrunch, CNBC hard data
  • Physical scarcity, not glut. Memory sold out through 2026 with server DRAM contract prices up ~270% this year; CoWoS booked; Blackwell allocation reserved into 2027; GE Vernova taking turbine reservations for 2031. Bubbles usually show the reverse. TrendForce, Utility Dive hard data
  • Valuations are earnings-backed at the top. Nvidia trades at 17.6× to 23.3× forward earnings depending on the fiscal-year convention, 26.8× trailing, with $193B of trailing net income. Cisco peaked near 131× forward in March 2000. The S&P 500's forward P/E compressed from 22.5× to 19.1× this year because earnings grew 30%. FactSet hard data
  • The capex is still guided up. Combined 2026 guidance rose from roughly $500B in February to about $730B, and every raise came with "demand exceeds supply" language. Broadcom's AI revenue grew 221% with a 46% free-cash-flow margin. Broadcom hard data
  • Adoption is broadening. Copilot paid seats rose from 20M to 30M in one quarter; Dell cites more than 6,500 AI customers; Super Micro's billion-dollar customers went from four to nine. hard data
  • Market-implied odds are low. Polymarket prices "AI bubble bursts by Dec 31, 2026" at 15.8%, down from ~25% in June; broad high-yield spreads at 271 bp show no systemic contagion. Morgan Stanley's Shalett: the build-out is "mostly rate-insensitive." Polymarket hard data / opinion

What "popping" would actually look like

The dot-com collapse was a 12 to 18 month stage progression, not a day. Price broke first and was noisy; the confirmations were revenue misses in late 2000 and the capex cliff in early 2001. That sequence is the template for the daily check.
