# Retrospective, 2026-11-04: seven weeks of the daily check

Covers the 37 runs from the Sep 15 baseline through Nov 4 (35 briefs, 100 logged events). Three questions: did the data sources work, did the signals measure anything, and how did the stage calls hold up against what happened next.

## 1. Data sources

How often each source was flagged as blocked, stale, unverified or not found in the briefs' Data quality sections (35 briefs):

| Source | Flagged | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| FRED (HY OAS, term premium, breakeven, DGS10) | 35 of 35 | Broken from the cloud environment and intermittently from this machine. C3, F3 and F4 have been carried values for most of the period. |
| Oracle CDS | 34 of 35 | Structurally unavailable: CDS prices belong to S&P Global, LSEG and ICE. The only usable prints came when Reuters or CNBC quoted one (Oct 15-19). C1 was stale 95% of the time. |
| GDELT | 30 of 35 | Self-inflicted: the routine fires two calls back to back and GDELT allows one every five seconds. |
| FINRA TRACE | 15 of 35 | Never produced a print. The public grid is JavaScript, and its data API rejects unauthenticated calls. SoftBank's new notes and CoreWeave's bonds were never read directly. |
| CoreWeave bonds and CDS | 13 of 35 | Same problem as Oracle: wire quotes only. C5's red rests on an Oct 14-16 print that was never refreshed. |
| CME FedWatch | 11 of 35 | Not fetchable; snippets were inconsistent and undated. F5 has been carried since the Sep 16 FOMC. |
| aibubblemonitor.com | 8 of 35 | Fixed Oct 16 by reading its JSON endpoint. |
| Google Trends | 7 of 35 | Rate-limited; E2 fell back to a Google News RSS fill-time measure, which is a hack. |
| Ramp AI Index | 6 of 35 | Not updated on FRED since August. G4 cannot be scored from it. |
| whalequant, Yahoo | 3 of 35 each | Occasional timeouts; fine. |
| Polymarket, boomorbubble.ai, aibubble.watch, SEC filings, company IR | 0 to 1 | Reliable. |

Fixes applied today: Treasury's daily yield-curve CSV as the FRED fallback for the 10-year, 2-year, 30-year and the breakeven; a six-second gap between GDELT calls; G3 scored from the latest filed 10-Q rather than waiting for the next one (the routine had Nvidia's August filing available for seven weeks and never read it).

Still unsolved: broad high-yield spreads and the term premium have no non-FRED source, so they stay carried when FRED fails. Bond prices need FINRA's API, which requires a free registration and a key; with it, C1 could use Oracle's bond spread as its primary measure, C5 could read CoreWeave's notes daily, and C4 could test every new deal's first-week trading. CDS will remain a wire-quote signal.

## 3. Stage calls against what followed

| Call | Stage | Next 15 trading days: S&P | SOX | Oracle | CoreWeave | HY spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sep 15 | 1, price break | +2.5% | +17.9% | | | +34 bp |
| Sep 21 | 0+, stress building | +2.1% | +5.5% | -4.2% | +0.9% | +45 bp |
| Oct 15 | 1, price break | -1.9% | -9.3% | -3.3% | -18.3% | +33 bp |
| Oct 16 | 5, credit event | -2.3% | -8.5% | -6.2% | -22.9% | +33 bp |

From the Sep 14 close to the Nov 3 close: S&P 500 -0.2%, Nasdaq-100 -2.6%, SOX -5.1%, Nvidia -2.6%, Oracle -13%, CoreWeave -35%, HY OAS 276 to 344 bp, VIX 17 to 21, 10-year 5.00% to 4.95%.

Reading: the September Stage 1 call was followed by a 18% semiconductor rally, which is what the framework says Stage 1 calls do. The October calls were directionally right for the leveraged layer, with CoreWeave down a fifth and Oracle down in the following three weeks. But the label "credit event" overstated it. No issuer defaulted, broad high-yield spreads widened 68 bp rather than blowing out, every hyperscaler raised capex, Oracle placed 20 billion dollars of stock and notes on Nov 2, and the two prints behind the call were never refreshed. The stage rules let a single-name print jump the framework from Stage 1 to Stage 5 without passing through 2, 3 or 4. The dot-com template is sequential; this cycle's leverage sits at the edge, in neoclouds, Oracle and SoftBank, so credit stress shows up early and locally. The rule should require breadth for Stage 5.

What the framework got right: the SoftBank bond as the funding-window test, although "oversubscribed means green" was too generous and secondary trading was the real test; OpenAI's round staying open and being repriced; Oracle's CDS and CoreWeave's bonds crossing their lines weeks after their equities had already said so; and the repeated insistence that Stage 4 needs an earnings call, which kept the verdict honest through three red weeks in October.

What it missed: politics and power. State moratoria in Texas, Ohio and Georgia became the dominant risk to the buildout in one night and no signal covers permitting, interconnection or electricity-price politics. Equity issuance as a funding tell: Oracle's 10 billion dollar stock sale is the kind of thing the funding-window pillar should catch, and E4 only watches IPOs and insider sales. The oil shock as the macro driver: F4 caught it partially.

## 4. Recommendations, for decision

Plumbing, done today: Treasury fallback, GDELT spacing, G3 from filings, the concerns ledger and Retro section.

Re-rules, proposed:

1. Stage 5 requires breadth: two of C1, C3, C4, C5 red, or C3 above 500 bp, or a default or going-concern notice. A single-name red is reported as "Stage N with credit stress in the leveraged layer".
2. Hysteresis for threshold signals A1, A2, A3, A5, F1, F2, F3: a color changes only after two consecutive closes past the line, and "changed" entries are not logged for the macro amplifier's daily moves.
3. C4 scores on first-week secondary trading, not the order book.

Sources, proposed:

4. Register for FINRA's API and make bond prices the primary measure for C1, C4 and C5, with CDS as the wire-quoted secondary.
5. Register for a FRED API key as a second route; keep the Treasury CSV as the fallback.
6. Replace F5's FedWatch dependence with a mechanical proxy, the 2-year yield minus the policy rate from the Treasury CSV, with FedWatch as commentary.
7. Replace E2's RSS fill-time measure with spaced GDELT calls, or score it weekly.

Signals, proposed:

8. New B5, policy and power: moratoria enacted or proposed in the top five data-center states, interconnection and large-load rule changes, permit denials, electricity-price actions. Red when a company confirms a halted project.
9. E4 becomes "equity issuance and insider selling": secondary offerings, converts and warrants by AI infrastructure names, which show the window is open and at what discount.
10. D4 uses the Census Business Trends survey's AI-use rate as its numeric primary, with ROI studies as commentary.
11. G4 is replaced: Microsoft does not disclose the Azure split and the Ramp index has stopped updating. Use the audited quarterly revenue and margin of listed labs, starting with Anthropic's filings, plus reported OpenAI run-rates, against their compute commitments.
12. Remove nothing yet. The confirmations that never fired are the point of the framework.

Process, proposed:

13. Run after the US close, around 22:30 UTC, instead of 6 AM Pacific, so each row reflects the same day's close and after-hours earnings. Every brief so far has described the previous day.
